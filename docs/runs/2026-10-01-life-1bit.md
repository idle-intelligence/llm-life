# Does Game of Life survive 1-bit and ternary weights?

Question: following the full-precision minimal-network sweep
(`docs/runs/2026-09-30-minimal-life.md`), does the Conway B3/S23 rule still
train exactly when the forward pass uses quantization-aware training (QAT)
with binary (sign, ±1) or ternary (BitNet b1.58-style, {-1,0,1}) weights
instead of full-precision floats?

References:
- Ma, Wang, Ma, Wang, Wang, Huang, Wei, "The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large
  Language Models are in 1.58 Bits" (BitNet b1.58), arXiv:2402.17764 —
  absmean ternary quantization scheme used here.
- Rastegari, Ordonez, Redmon, Farhadi, "XNOR-Net: ImageNet Classification
  Using Binary Convolutional Neural Networks", arXiv:1603.05279 — sign-based
  binary weight quantization with a per-layer scale, used here for the
  binary variant.

## Setup

- New code: `pytorch/minimal_life_1bit.py`. Reuses `pytorch/life.py`
  (`Grid`/`Rule`) and the eval/plateau conventions from
  `pytorch/minimal_life.py`/`minimal_life_full.py`.
- Quantization: latent full-precision weights `w`; forward pass uses a
  straight-through estimator `w_ste = w + (wq - w).detach()`, so the forward
  value is `wq` but the gradient flows to `w` as identity.
  - **Ternary** (BitNet b1.58): per-layer scale `gamma = mean(|w|)`,
    `wq = clamp(round(w / gamma), -1, 1)`, output scaled by `gamma` once per
    layer.
  - **Binary** (XNOR-Net style): per-layer scale `gamma = mean(|w|)`,
    `wq = sign(w)` (zero mapped to +1), output scaled by `gamma`.
  - Biases stay full precision (standard practice in both papers).
  - Activations: full precision only (ReLU, sigmoid) — the
    binary/ternary-activation variant was not run; see Observations.
- Model families, same architectures as the full-precision sweep:
  - **QuantCNN**: `QuantConv3x3(1->c, circular padding, bias) -> ReLU ->
    QuantConv1x1(c->1, bias)`. c in {2, 4, 8, 16}.
  - **QuantMLP**: 9 neighbourhood values -> `QuantLinear(9,h) -> ReLU ->
    QuantLinear(h,1)`. h in {4, 8, 16, 32}.
- Training: Adam (0.9, 0.999, eps 1e-8), lr 3e-3, batch 32, board size
  16x16, training density d0 = 0.38 (the best full-precision density).
  Up to 20,000 steps, plateau stop (window 10 evals of 200 steps each,
  stop when best score hasn't improved by 0.5%). 10 seeds per condition.
- Exactness criterion, evaluated with ONLY the quantized weights (plus the
  per-layer scale): 100% on all 512 3x3 neighbourhood cases and 100% of
  cells on 100 fresh random 16x16 boards, same as the full-precision sweep.
- Compute: the RTX 3080 machine, one `systemd-run --user` unit, GPU confirmed idle beforehand.

## Integer operations per output cell (quantized forward only)

Counting one add/subtract per nonzero weight (ternary's zero weights need
no op), plus one add per bias, excluding the two per-layer scale
multiplies (one float multiply each, applied once per layer, not per
weight) and the ReLU/sigmoid nonlinearities:

| model | nonzero weight adds/subs | bias adds | total int ops/cell | float multiplies |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| QuantCNN c=4 ternary | 26 | 5 | 31 | 2 |
| QuantMLP h=4 ternary | 28 | 5 | 33 | 2 |
| QuantCNN c=16 binary | 160 | 17 | 177 | 2 |

The binary model's op count equals its full parameter count exactly,
since XNOR-style weights are never zero — every weight contributes an
add or subtract. The ternary model's op count (31, 45 params) is below
its parameter count because 19 of its 45 weights/biases quantized to
exactly zero and need no operation.

## Observations

- Ternary (BitNet b1.58-style) survives Game of Life at the same
  reliability the full-precision sweep found, but needs roughly double the
  parameters: ternary CNN c=16 and MLP h=16 (177 params each) both hit
  10/10, where full precision reached 10/10 already at 89 params (CNN c=8,
  MLP h=8). Ternary at 89 params (c=8 / h=8) only reaches 5/10, and at 45
  params (c=4 / h=4) only 2/10 — both strictly worse than the full-precision
  convergence rate at the identical architecture and parameter count (7/10
  and 10/10 respectively). Quantizing to ternary costs convergence
  reliability at a fixed width; the fix is to go wider, not to retrain
  longer (budget here was already 20k steps with a generous plateau
  window, well above the full-precision sweep's 2.5k-step budget).
- Binary (XNOR-Net style, no zero state) is substantially harder: no width
  up to c=8/h=8 (89 params) converged at all, and even c=16/h=16/h=32 (177
  and 353 params) only reach 6/10, 2/10 and 1/10 respectively — never
  reliable in this sweep. The qualitative claim from the interim report
  ("binary never converges") does not hold at the widest sizes tried, but
  binary is clearly the harder regime: it needs roughly 2x the ternary
  width to get any converged seeds, and never approaches the 10/10
  reliability ternary or full precision reach.
- Several non-converged binary conditions (CNN c=2/c=4/c=8, MLP h=4)
  plateau at exactly 0.890625 cells-correct. This is not a noise
  coincidence: 0.890625 = 456/512 is the exact accuracy of the trivial
  rule "predict alive iff exactly 3 of the 9 cells in the 3x3 neighbourhood
  (self included) are alive" on the full 512-case truth table (checked by
  brute force over all 512 cases). That rule is the natural local optimum
  for a binary (sign, no-zero, equal-magnitude) linear unit: since every
  weight has the same magnitude and sign bit, the unit cannot down-weight
  or zero out the self cell relative to the 8 neighbours, so it can only
  threshold on total population count. A pure population-count-3 threshold
  reproduces Life's birth rule (dead cell, exactly 3 live neighbours, total
  3) and one of its two survival cases (live cell, exactly 2 live
  neighbours, total 3) but misses the other survival case (live cell,
  exactly 3 live neighbours, total 4) — exactly the "live cell, 3 live
  neighbours" case, which is C(8,3) = 56 of the 512 table entries, giving
  512 - 56 = 456 matches (0.890625). Every other case agrees with the real
  rule, since total != 3 is dead under both rules and total == 3 is alive
  under both except for that one case. Ternary's zero weight is what
  breaks this trap: it lets a unit null out the self term (or a specific
  neighbour) and recover the self/neighbour asymmetry the real rule needs,
  which is consistent with ternary converging far more often than binary
  at every matched width.
- The smallest converged ternary CNN (QuantCNN c=4, 45 params) and MLP
  (QuantMLP h=4, 45 params) are both close to human-readable. The CNN's
  first conv1 filter (all +1 except a 0 center) is a pure neighbour-count
  detector with the self cell correctly nulled to zero by the ternary
  quantization; its fourth filter (all -1, self included) is a negated
  total-population detector. conv2 combines `-relu(filter0) - relu(filter3)`
  plus a bias, i.e. "more negative as both the neighbour count and the
  total population rise" with a single threshold — a monotone-in-count
  readout that reproduces the exact birth/survival boundary through the
  bias terms rather than needing distinct rules per filter. The MLP's
  version is more directly readable: row0 is the 8-neighbour sum (self
  excluded, weight exactly 0 on the self input) with a large negative bias
  (-2.124), and row3 is the negative sum of all 9 inputs including self
  with a matching positive bias (+2.125) — together they split the
  decision into "how many neighbours" and "am I currently alive", the same
  two facts the real rule needs, recovered purely from ternary-quantized
  gradient descent.
- The binary/ternary-activation variant ("a second variant with
  binary/ternary activations too, if the first works") was not run: the
  ternary-weight variant converged reliably at c=16/h=16, so it qualifies
  as "works", but the sweep's generous step budget already consumed the
  compute time available this session; it is the natural next step if this
  line of inquiry continues.
