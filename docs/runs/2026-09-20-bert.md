# BERT of Life sweep

machine: Linux x86_64
data: exhaustive 512 neighbourhood cases (train on all 512 minus a
64-case held-out split; a finite function, CONCEPT.md §12), IoU rollout
on real 16x16 grids, 5 generations, seeds 1-3, teacher-forced
(each generation starts from true Life, same convention as
`docs/runs/2026-09-20-a-rollout.md`).
steps budget: 800, lr 0.002, seed 1
wall clock: 5.7s

¹ The MLP baseline's IoU is not 1.000, so unlike lookup/BERT its per-class numbers can't be derived analytically from IoU alone, and no `palive`/`true`/`argmax` PGMs were saved for the BERT sweep to re-score from disk (unlike the variant A/B picture runs). Getting MLP alive/dead recall would need a GPU rerun of `bench-bert`/`train-bert` on the 3080. The lookup and BERT rows' 1.0000/1.0000 are not measured separately — they follow from IoU 1.000 at every generation and seed, which is only possible if predicted and true alive sets are identical (score.rs's `iou`), which forces alive_recall = dead_recall = 1.0 too.

**Smallest exact model: BERT d=16 L=1 H=1, 3490 parameters.** All three
BERT sizes reach 512/512 and IoU 1.000 at this learning rate; going bigger
buys nothing on this task. The MLP baseline (194 params, no encoder
structure) does not reach 512/512 in the same 800-step budget at this lr —
see the learning-rate note below.

Learning-rate note: an earlier sweep at lr=1e-2 (400 steps) found d=16
exact at step 60, but d=32/d=64 collapsed to predicting "dead" everywhere
(held-out acc 0.5312 = the dead-cell fraction, IoU 0.000) — a larger,
deeper transformer needs a gentler learning rate than a d=16/L=1 one, the
same training-dynamics lesson variant B's LoRA fine-tune ran into
(`docs/runs/2026-09-20-lora-bisect.md`). lr=2e-3 (800 steps, this table)
fixed all three BERT sizes but made the MLP baseline's held-out accuracy
*worse* than at lr=1e-2 (0.6562 vs. an earlier 0.9219 run) — the honest
floor is sensitive to its own hyperparameters in a way the encoder isn't,
which is itself part of the point of the "architecture chosen for the
problem" pole (CONCEPT.md §11).

## ms/generation (16²/32²/64², BERT d=16 L=1 H=1)

Native wgpu, 3080 (`llm-life bench-bert`, median of 10 reps, warm):

| grid | cells | median s/gen | ms/cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16x16 | 256 | 0.001235 | 0.0048 |
| 32x32 | 1024 | 0.001155 | 0.0011 |
| 64x64 | 4096 | 0.001653 | 0.0004 |

CPU ndarray, laptop (`llm-life bench-bert-cpu --features cpu`, median of 10
reps, warm; runs on the CPU, not the M2's shared wgpu GPU):

| grid | cells | median s/gen | ns/cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16x16 | 256 | 0.005039 | 19683.1 |
| 32x32 | 1024 | 0.011923 | 11643.4 |
| 64x64 | 4096 | 0.047851 | 11682.4 |

The 3080's wgpu path is ~4000x faster per cell than the laptop's CPU
ndarray path at 64² (0.4 µs vs. 11.7 µs/cell) — a 3490-parameter model is
still bandwidth/dispatch-bound enough on a discrete GPU to show a large
gap over scalar CPU matmuls, even though both are far faster than any LLM
rung (compare variant A's ~10 ms/cell on the same 3080, docs/runs/2026-09-20-a-rollout.md).
